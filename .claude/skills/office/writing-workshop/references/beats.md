## A chosen journey through a fixed pile

Read the full Markdown pile through EOF. This is **exploit**: exploration is done, the
material is fixed, and we choose a path through it. Keep the pile read-only and write to a
different exact authorized article path, rejecting canonical-path and device/inode aliases.
Paraphrase, split, recombine or quote supplied material as the selected beat needs. The pile
is a quarry; it does not prescribe either the order or how much must be used.

## Confirm prerequisites before any beats

Settle with the real author what the audience knows on arrival. This is the initial
**grounded** set. An actual answer is required; inferred expertise is still a proposal.
Use already confirmed prerequisites rather than asking again merely because a turn changed.

A concept is grounded only as a confirmed prerequisite or as an idea actually introduced
by a still-present earlier beat. If it has a name, establish both idea and term. The unit
is the concept, not the jargon: a seemingly ordinary phrase can still rely on an unknown
idea. Track the set explicitly in the session, not as metadata in the article.

Each beat **requires** prior concepts and **grounds** new ones. It is reachable only when
all its requires belong to the current grounded set: `requires ⊆ grounded`. Picking a beat
that establishes X unlocks later directions needing X. Say which new grounds and later
directions each choice offers, so the author can see a little way down the path.

Prerequisite versus introduced concept is an editorial trade-off. Demanding too much up
front excludes readers; teaching too much early can drown the opening in definitions. If
an attractive beat needs an ungrounded concept, first offer a reachable grounding beat,
or ask the author to explicitly change the prerequisite. Never silently promote a concept
to prerequisite or offer the ungrounded jump as if it were reachable.

## Offer, choose, write one, stop

1. After prerequisite confirmation, offer **2–3 candidate starting beats** drawn from the
   real pile, each a different entry point. Show the candidate text/move, `requires`,
   `grounds`, and **unlocks** (which subsequent direction would become reachable).
   Every offered starting beat must be reachable. Keep the proposal in the conversation,
   outside the article file, and wait for the author to pick exactly one.
2. Re-read the actual article and current preimage. Once the real author chooses, write
   **only that beat** to the authorized target. **STOP**. The choice grants no subsequent
   beat, whole article or speculative branch write.
3. Re-read the article from disk before the next proposals. Reconstruct what it establishes
   and offer **2–3 reachable next beats**, again with requires/grounds/unlocks. These are
   different pivots from where the reader now stands, not reordered labels for one move.
4. Wait for one real selection; re-read before writing just that beat; STOP again. Repeat
   the journey until it reaches its natural end. An unanswered or ambiguous choice means
   no write. If actual material supports fewer than two options, name the shortage and
   ask for supporting material or the author's direction rather than invent candidates.

The selected beat's grounds enter the grounded set only after it is actually written.
An unselected candidate teaches the reader nothing. A general “keep going” is not a choice
between unresolved beats; ask which reachable move the author wants.

## Size a beat by its one move

A beat makes one move: set a scene, land a point, ask a question, drop an aside or twist
the angle. Then it stops and leaves room for a pivot. Its size is what that move needs:

- One sentence when that is enough (“And then nothing happened for three weeks.”).
- A short paragraph when it needs setup.
- Several paragraphs for one self-contained vignette, argument or example.

Five paragraphs plus three subheadings usually means two moves glued together: split the
proposal into distinct beats before asking for a selection. Length alone is not the test;
the test is whether it is one coherent move. Do not add labels, requires/grounds tables,
dates, unrequested frontmatter or platform metadata to the article to simulate grounding.
Those are decision aids for the conversation/handoff, not part of the author's prose.

## Preserve edits; recompute the path

Append one selected beat at a time, never write ahead, and re-read disk before every write.
Preserve user edits absolutely. A substantive rewrite, deletion or rollback changes what
the reader now knows: rebuild introduced concepts from the current article plus confirmed
prerequisites, removing foundations no longer established. Let that change next candidates.

“Rewrite that beat” or “go back and try another beat 3” means edit that specified beat in
place and leave all other bytes alone. If the location or intended replacement is unclear,
ask rather than guessing. Then recompute grounding; flag dependencies affected in later
beats and wait for the author's decision before touching those beats.

Missing example, quote or foundation means real additional material or an agreed cut; no
fabricated event, citation or simulated user response. The original material file remains
unchanged throughout the journey.

## End at the article's answer, not an empty quarry

The journey ends when it has answered the article's question. Confirm that natural end with
the author; unused fragments are normal and can remain unused. Do not automatically write
all leftover pile material or append a recap merely to exhaust it. Report the actual article
and unresolved decisions using the shared completion/handoff rules.
