## Start with what the author already said

This is pure **explore**: widen what could be written before committing to structure.
Capture usable fragments from the very first user message, including the initial prompt.
Once topic and exact output authorization are known, use that material immediately rather
than making the author repeat it. Missing topic/path means one input question and waiting;
retain the real initial material until the path is answered, never invent a starter fragment.

Interview the author closely about what they want to write about. Follow the concrete
noticing, event, comparison, tension or half-thought in their last answer. Ask one useful
question and wait; the next question responds to the real answer. A grilling-style interview
here mines observations, not a Plan, phase sequence, outline or finished article structure.

As fragments emerge from either side, append them to one Markdown file in discovery order.
Distinguish the author's facts, a proposed phrasing, and what still needs verification. An
agent's suggestion may become a fragment but is not an invented event or quotation.

## What survives as a fragment

A fragment is a piece of text that might survive into the final article. It must be readable
to the author; it need not define every term or be self-contained for a cold reader. Ask
whether it is a piece of good writing, rather than whether it is a complete argument.

Keep heterogeneous material:

- A sharp sentence whose eventual location is still unknown.
- A claim with a one-line justification.
- A vignette, real event, code snippet, scenario or analogy.
- A half-thought to work out later.
- A supplied quotation, dialogue or overheard line, attributed to its real source.
- A list of observations that belong together by feel.
- A complaint, confession or punchline.
- A **leading word**, a compact metaphor or coinage that names a recurring pattern.

The leading word is especially valuable: it can carry the later title, structure and
transitions. When repeated observations circle one idea, press for a compact name that
captures them. Test that word against the author's actual examples; don't impose a slogan
because it sounds clever. The model is a novelist's diary of unstructured noticings that can
later be mined. Exploration does not have to make every fragment fit a thesis.

## One H1, then fragments

The first write uses a single working-title H1 at the top and the available fragment body.
There is no metadata, frontmatter, TOC or date. The title may change when the author asks.
Separate fragments with a horizontal rule (`\n---\n`), retaining the order they were added.
No body headings, tags, labels or organizing outline. Lists, code and quotes may occur
inside a fragment when that is its natural shape. A prose indication that a fact remains
unverified preserves uncertainty; it is not an invitation to add metadata or tagging.

```markdown
# Working title

A sentence or a small vignette the author wants to keep.

It may run to more than one paragraph.

---

> A real supplied quotation.

An observation about it.

---

- Related observations
- That hang together by feel
```

## Capture rhythm and precise revisions

Append quietly within the authorized capture session. A brief “adding that” is enough;
do not interrupt each fragment with a save dialog. Re-read the actual file before every
append, because the author may have edited, reordered or deleted fragments between turns.
Do not reconstruct the file from a cached version or restore material the author cut.

“Cut the last one”, “rewrite that one sharper” and “merge those two” are first-class
instructions. Identify the specified fragment(s) in the current disk version, then make
only that requested cut/rewrite/merge. Preserve every other byte and position. Ask once
which fragment if the target is ambiguous; don't guess or normalize neighboring fragments.

Finish or pause when the author requests it. No automatic outline, shape session or beat
journey follows. If they explicitly choose an exploit mode later, bind its read-only
material and distinct article path under the shared skill contract.
