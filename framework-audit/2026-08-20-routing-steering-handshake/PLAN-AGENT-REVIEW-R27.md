## The architectural deletion

**Question 1 — does it still close E1?** Yes, for the case it was designed for. Tracing §0.1's chain: `进入luca app项目` → §4.2's scan finds the literal alias `luca app` adjacent to `项目`, producing `alias_resolution.candidates=[{surface:"luca app",canonical:"muse",...,marker_present:true}]` (lines 515–523, 554). This is non-authorizing evidence only. §5.2/§8.1's classification is at the LLM layer, not the hook: the LLM reads the candidate plus the actual sentence and decides to switch. Nothing in §5.2's obligation-creation logic (616–761) or §8.1's `NEW_TASK_SIGNAL_WITH_PROJECT` two-region composition (706–723, 3438–3454, 3542–3545) requires the hook itself to have authorized anything — they require only that a canonical target be resolvable and that the classifier (which is explicitly LLM/model-level per the routing contract this plan invokes) recognize the combined phrase. **Question 2 — did deleting the grammar strand anything?** Yes, and this is the BLOCKER. The deletion was not completed uniformly across the document:

1. **§4.2 itself contradicts its own headline claim.** Lines 481–500 (the "Executable selection grammar" bullets) are *not* deleted — they still define clause-terminal, trailing-content-disarming, "authorizes aliases" / "makes the clause non-authorizing" logic (a metadata alias's marker "must be clause-terminal except for a bounded polite particle," and "a following possessive/content token... makes the clause non-authorizing," lines 486–494). This is the exact old mechanical-verdict architecture. Two paragraphs later (503–523), the plan asserts the opposite: `RESOLVE` "never authorizes," and the hook's only operation is "scan the raw prompt for occurrences of a registered canonical directory ID or metadata alias... Quoting, backticking, negation, questions, report framing and **clause structure are not consulted**" (520). These two descriptions of the same mechanism are incompatible: one says trailing content and clause-terminal position gate whether a mention becomes a candidate; the other says only marker-adjacency matters and clause structure is irrelevant.

**BLOCKER-1 — §4.2/§5.1's deletion is incomplete and self-contradictory, and §8.1 structurally depends on the deleted mechanism.**
- Contract conflict: §4.2 lines 481–500 and 558–561 assert an "authorizes/non-authorizing," clause-terminal, trailing-content-disarming grammar; §4.2 lines 503, 515–523 assert the opposite — pure marker-adjacency scanning with "clause structure... not consulted." §5.1 lines 598–600 assert `NEG`/`ADV`/`NEG_SUSPECT` "exist... nowhere in this section"; §5.1 lines 611–614 demand fixtures enumerated over `NEG` members, `ADV` members and a "fail-closed arm" that do not exist. §8.1 lines 3522–3523 require a negation-scoping step "the same... as §4.2/§5.1" that §4.2/§5.1 no longer define.

Independently, an implementer following §8.1 line 3522 literally cannot build "the same... negation spans as §4.2/§5.1" because there is nothing left to import.
- Minimum repair: (a) in §4.2, either delete bullets 481–500 and the trailing-content clause of 558–561 to match the pure-scan description, or delete the pure-scan claim at 515–523 and keep the retained grammar — pick one, not both, and update the required-examples list to match; (b) delete §5.1 lines 611–614's `NEG`/`ADV`/fail-closed-arm fixture demands (they belong to §14's already-correct `R-SIGNAL` block, which does not need them); (c) rewrite §8.1 line 3522–3523 to describe only what actually remains — clause/quote/backtick/report/example-span filtering, with negation handled at the LLM layer via `negation_context`, not a "negation spans" import from §4.2/§5.1.
